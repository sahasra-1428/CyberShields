    How to Identify Spoofed & Phishing Email Addresses

    Mail Integrity Guide

    1. Anatomy of a Spoofed Email Address

    "PayPal Security Desk" < urgent-alert @ paypa1 .com >
    Display Name ("PayPal Security") Free text that anyone can set in Outlook or Gmail to trick your eyes.
    urgent-alert (Mailbox) Psychological urgency keyword in the username.
    paypa1 (Fake Domain) Substitutes letter 'l' with number '1' to mimic PayPal.
    .com (Top-Level Domain) The actual domain registered by the attacker.

    2. Critical Email Security Myths Debunked

    ✉️ Myth 1: "The sender name says Apple Support, so it must be Apple."

    Reality: The Display Name is purely cosmetic. Anyone can configure their email client to display "Google Security", "SBI Bank", or "Police Cyber Cell" while sending from a completely unrelated domain like `random_user@xyz-mail.com`.

    📥 Myth 2: "It landed in my Primary Inbox, so it must be verified safe."

    Reality: Scammers frequently use freshly registered domains with clean IP histories or compromise legitimate small business email accounts to bypass initial spam filters. Inbox delivery is never proof of security.

    3. The 5 Heuristic Pillars Inspected by CyberShield

    1. Domain Typosquatting Swaps

    Swapping characters: paypa1.com, arnazon.com, micros0ft.com.

    Defense: Check the exact spelling after the @ symbol.
    2. Disposable / Burner Mailboxes

    Free throwaway inboxes: mailinator.com, 10minutemail, tempmail.

    Defense: Reputable institutions never communicate from anonymous burner domains.
    3. Subdomain Stacking in Mail

    Pretending to be Amazon: billing@amazon.orders-verification.com.

    Defense: The real domain is orders-verification.com, NOT amazon.com.
    4. Public Free Mail for Corporate Claims

    Official bank or police claiming from sbi_official_helpdesk@gmail.com.

    Defense: Banks never use free consumer webmail (Gmail, Yahoo) for notices.
    5. RFC 5322 Syntax Compliance

    Detecting illegal characters, malformed headers, and missing TLD suffixes.

    Defense: Enforces standard Internet message formatting rules.

    4. Four-Step Email Authentication Checklist

    1. Always expand the sender details: Click the down arrow next to the sender to see the real email between < >.
    2. Check the domain after the @: Look for transposed characters (rn vs m) and unusual extensions (.xyz, .top).
    3. Never trust free webmail for organizations: If a job offer or bank notice comes from @gmail.com or @yahoo.com, treat it as fraudulent.
    4. Never download unsolicited attachments: Files with extensions like .html, .iso, .zip, or .vbs often deliver malware payloads.
